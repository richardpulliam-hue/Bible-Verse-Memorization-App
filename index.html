The method
Work left to right. Learn breaks the verse into phrases, since you memorize a phrase at a time, not a word at a time. Fade removes words in stages so your memory has to supply more of the verse each pass. Letters is the first-letter drill: type the first letter of each word, with three tries before the word is shown. Recall is the real test, the whole verse and reference from a blank page. After each session, grade yourself. A verse you know moves to a longer interval (1, 3, 7, then 14 days); one you miss comes back today. Testing yourself and spacing the reviews is what moves a verse into long-term memory, far more than rereading it. Everyone in the family signs in with their own name and PIN, so each person's schedule follows them to any phone, tablet or laptop. The family board shows who has practiced today, each person's streak in days, and how far along each verse is: the darker the square, the longer the review interval. A verse counts as mastered once its interval reaches 14 days.
Keeping verses fresh
Once a verse reaches a 14-day interval it moves to Kept. Kept verses keep coming back, but further apart each time (30, 60, 120, then 180 days), because a memory recalled just as it starts to fade lasts longer than one reviewed while it is still easy. Fresh review mixes your kept verses together and tests each one a different way: recite it from the reference, name the reference from its opening words, or recite it from first letters only. Mixing and switching directions keeps you from leaning on one cue. If a verse is shaky, it goes back to Learning for a review tomorrow, and one good review returns it to Kept. The freshness bar under each kept verse empties as its next review gets closer.
